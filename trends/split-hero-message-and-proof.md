# Split hero pairs message with proof

Status: `stable` (Evergreen)

## Observation

In a 2,211-site curated dataset with uniform layout annotation, 51.3% of sites open with a split hero (left/right two-column pairing of message block and visual evidence). The share holds between 42% and 66% across 15 of 16 site categories — agency 44.0%, e-commerce 42.0%, AI 49.5%, web apps 59.1%, development tools 62.3%, finance 66.0% — and stayed statistically flat between the June 2025 baseline batch and sites added July 2025 – August 2026 (50.1% → 44.5%, ns), while surface vocabulary (imagery, color, motion) shifted 20–40pp over the same batches.

## Mechanism

The split hero solves a stable compression problem: deliver the value proposition and the product evidence simultaneously in one viewport. Text carries the claim, visual carries the proof, and neither pushes the other below the fold. The mechanism survives style cycles because the problem — claim plus demonstration above the fold — does not change with fashion.

## Fit

Product, SaaS, finance, dev-tool and commerce heroes where claim and demonstration must coexist; any launch page that must both say and show.

## Avoid

Pure brand or atmosphere pieces where one immersive statement matters more than explanation. Forcing a split when there is no meaningful proof asset produces filler imagery; when the message itself is the experience, centered or fullscreen treatments carry it better.

## Evidence

- Dataset measurement: 2,208 uniformly annotated heroes; prevalence, cross-category transfer and batch stability as above (layout annotation by remote visual model, single annotation pass).
- [Mckp.live](https://mckp.live/) — verified instance: message left, photoreal device proof right; cover visually checked 2026-09-10.

## Uncertainty

The dataset is cross-sectional; longevity is inferred from batch persistence plus the mechanism's long-standing convention status, not from dated design history. Prevalence measures curated practice, not the whole web.
