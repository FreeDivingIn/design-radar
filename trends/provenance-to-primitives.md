# Provenance becomes a reusable primitive

Status: `growing`

## Observation

Across distinct June–September batches, identities repeatedly turn project-specific evidence into reusable system parts instead of stopping at a heritage mood.

- **Rapha** built six bespoke type families from cycling magazines, race handouts, jerseys and technical manuals, then tested them across apparel, editorial, digital and motion.
- **Horniman Museum** foraged letterforms from its collections, architecture and gardens to build a growing campaign alphabet.
- **The Rampant Club** converts heraldry, tartan and whisky-glass facets into its mark, custom type and layout geometry.
- **The Kithmore** converts found signpainting and golf ephemera into bespoke letterforms, marks, illustration and repeat patterns.
- **Ytri** carries Træna-specific history, roofline/peak geometry and local materials into type, marks, signage and room numbers.

## Inference

Provenance is increasingly functioning as a generative constraint: a visual primitive is stronger when its source can be traced to the subject and still works across touchpoints. This is more specific than applying generic archival or retro styling.

## Fit

Place-, community-, craft- or culture-led brands with legitimate source material and enough touchpoints for it to become a system.

## Avoid

Thin provenance, utility products with no relevant history, or cultural references that risk caricature or uncredited borrowing.

## Uncertainty

The pattern is strongest in identity work with rich cultural or physical context. `growing` reflects recurrence across several recent batches, not a claim that provenance-led branding is new in itself.
