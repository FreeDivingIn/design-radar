# Top-bar navigation carries orientation

Status: `stable` (Evergreen)

## Observation

In the same 2,211-site dataset, 94.5% of annotated sites keep primary navigation in a top bar. The share never drops below 86.7% in any of the 16 site categories (portfolio lowest at 86.7%; tech, dev tools, web apps above 98%), and it is exactly stable across batches: 93.5% in the June 2025 baseline vs 93.6% in sites added July 2025 – August 2026.

## Mechanism

The top bar solves persistent orientation at near-zero learning cost: it sits at the conventional scanning edge, holds identity, wayfinding and the primary action in one predictable row, and stays visible without competing with content. Its durability comes from convention — users arrive with the model already formed, so the mechanism spends no attention budget on itself.

## Fit

Virtually all content and product sites, especially multi-section products where orientation and a persistent primary action matter.

## Avoid

Reading this as "never innovate navigation." Immersive or artistic one-pagers legitimately drop the bar (6.2% of the dataset shows no visible nav, concentrated in portfolio contexts). The mechanism's value is orientation, not the bar itself — alternate forms that preserve persistent orientation serve the same function.

## Evidence

- Dataset measurement as above; `none-visible` nav appears in 137/2,208 annotated sites, mostly portfolio — the exception that defines the norm.

## Uncertainty

Same cross-sectional caveat as other dataset-backed Evergreen entries: longevity inferred from batch persistence and convention status, not dated design history.
